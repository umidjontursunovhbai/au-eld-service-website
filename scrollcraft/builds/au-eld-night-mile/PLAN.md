# AU ELD Service · Night Mile build plan

## Grammar

**Continuous world.** One fixed stage and one Scrollcraft worldflight spacer. Nothing else enters document flow, so the truck, road and atmosphere never cut or unpin.

Why the other grammars lost:

- Filmic one-shot repeats the conventional fixed-bar and section handoff pattern.
- Chaptered editorial and typographic poster conflict with the client’s background-motion request.
- Live surface is already taken by the current build and reads too much like a dashboard.
- Gallery/catalog has no real product set to catalogue.
- Split stage turns the service into a comparison the client did not ask for.
- Rhythmic cutlist is too loud for “less distractions.”

## Journey and feeling curve

| Waypoint | Feeling | Worldflight behaviour | Track window |
|---|---|---|---:|
| Recognition | Confidence | Full logo, hero copy and the first visible piece of the AU Lane | hero |
| Included | Clarity | Three compact benefit groups arrive in sequence: driver desk, compliance and connected fleet | 0.13–0.54 |
| Shift watch | Reassurance | Human support leads; the three alert-assisted monitoring moments arrive one by one | 0.51–0.72 |
| Offer | Momentum | One week free, the monthly price and the service promise resolve into a rounded trial action | 0.69–0.88 |
| Contact exits | Readiness | The road settles low while the free-week invitation, returning mark and three direct contact choices resolve the journey | 0.84–1.00 |

Worldflight: four colour-matched 4.5-second photographic legs at 1.4vh each, plus the engine’s 1vh landing, total **6.6 viewport-heights**. The tighter track follows the requested faster feel while the legs still crossfade inside one fixed world and keep a useful scrub range.

## Signature move

**AU Lane.** A broad three-layer SVG road begins at the exact visual angle of the logo’s blue slash. Scroll reveals the cobalt road edge, asphalt centre and lane dashes; a truck marker follows the path. At the peak the route occupies the whole foreground. At the close the road settles below an asymmetric Dispatch Channel Console; wide screens receive three restrained route-map traces while mobile keeps a single clean lane.

This is not a thin decorative line. Its perspective width, asphalt centre, moving lane markings and light reflections make it the physical stage of the page.

## Motion score

- Hero: two-line opacity and lift settle with no glyph mask.
- Benefits: three copy windows with short list-item lifts; only one compact group holds focus at a time.
- Support: the lead moves as one block while alert rows arrive in a restrained horizontal stagger.
- Offer: kicker, free-week headline, price, action and service promise rise in a fast ordered sequence.
- World: scroll-linked photographic push-in from 1.015× to 1.12×, rain specks, reflected light and pointer-depth offset.
- Signature: SVG road draw, lane flow, truck path-follow and three sequential desktop route traces.
- Close: the free-week title overlaps the offer handoff while the three channels fade in and draw an internal signal trace inside one grouped console.
- Reduced motion: photographic poster, fully drawn road, stationary truck and reachable copy/contact windows without lateral motion; mobile/tablet omits the decorative traces.

## Implementation constraints

- Real semantic headings and links.
- No generic feature-card grid, form, fake dashboard, badges, counters, scroll cue or section numbers.
- Continuous work uses transform, opacity, clip-path and SVG stroke offset only.
- Contact values live in one `CONTACT` object.
- The new redesign stays unpushed until the client explicitly asks.
